Most AI coding assistant pilots are set up to produce one number: seat activation.
It's the wrong number for the meeting that decides whether the pilot continues.

Activation is easy to report and it usually looks healthy. But whoever signs the renewal isn't asking how many people logged in. They're asking what's different about the work.

This is common. In the Northfield Developer Survey 2026 (n=1,240 developers, fielded March 2026), 61% said their company's assistant pilot had "no agreed success measure" at launch.

No agreed measure at launch means no baseline. No baseline means that at day 90 you can show usage, but you can't show that anything about the work is different.

That's the stall. The model can be perfectly good and the pilot still produces no evidence either way. So the renewal turns into an argument about budget and gut feel.

The fix sits in week one, not the week before renewal. Before rollout, write down the one thing you expect to change and the person who owns that number.

An activation chart is a fine appendix slide. It shouldn't be the only evidence you bring when someone asks what changed.
