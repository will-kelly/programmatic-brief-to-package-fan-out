9 out of 9.
That's how many companies in one study renewed their AI coding assistant after tracking changed work during the pilot.

Halvorsen Research interviewed 32 companies for its May 2026 report on platform teams and AI tooling. The median pilot ran 94 days before a renewal decision.

9 of those 32 companies tracked any change in cycle time or review load during the pilot. All 9 renewed.

The other 23 tracked only seat activation. 11 of them renewed.

I don't read this as "measure it and the tool will work." A team that bothers to track review load may also have picked a sharper use case. And 32 interviews is a small sample, not a controlled trial.

What I do read is simpler. When the decision arrived, the 9 had evidence to put on the table. The 23 had a login count.

Activation tells you people opened the tool. It tells you nothing about whether the work moved, and that's the question the person signing the renewal is asking.

If your pilot is past day 30 and you can't name the one work metric you'll show at renewal, you're on track for a day-90 stall. Pick the metric this week.
