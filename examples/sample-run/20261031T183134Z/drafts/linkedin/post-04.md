Some AI coding assistant pilots should stall.
A work metric protects you from a bad renewal as much as it helps you win a good one.

I've spent this series arguing that pilots stall because teams measure seat activation instead of changed work. There's evidence that cuts the other way, and it deserves a straight hearing.

A 2025 Ostrava University study of 400 pull requests found no measurable change in review time with assistant use.

If review time is your success metric and it doesn't move, not renewing may be the right call. That's the pilot doing its job.

I'd hedge on how far that study goes. It's one study, 400 pull requests, and one metric. Review time isn't the only thing an assistant might change. But it's a fair warning against assuming the answer is always yes.

The activation-only approach is weakest here. In Halvorsen Research's 2026 report, 11 of the 23 companies that tracked only seat activation renewed. They renewed without evidence either way. Some of them may have bought something that doesn't change their work, and they have no number that would tell them.

A measured pilot that ends in "no" is a better result than an unmeasured pilot that ends in "yes."
