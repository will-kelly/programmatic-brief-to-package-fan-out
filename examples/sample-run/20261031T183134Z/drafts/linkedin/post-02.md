The CFO asked one question at the day-90 meeting: what changed?
The only slide on the screen was a seat-activation chart at 92%.

A platform lead at a logistics company described that meeting. It's the clearest picture I know of how these pilots end.

92% looks like a win. It's the number most pilots are set up to produce. But nobody in that room was asking how many people logged in.

This is common. In the Northfield Developer Survey 2026 (n=1,240 developers, fielded March 2026), 61% said their company's assistant pilot had "no agreed success measure" at launch.

No agreed measure at launch means no baseline. No baseline means that at day 90 you can show usage, but you can't show that anything about the work is different.

That's the stall. The model can be perfectly good and the pilot still produces no evidence either way. So the renewal turns into an argument about budget and gut feel.

The fix sits at day 1, not day 89. Before rollout, write down the one thing you expect to change and the person who owns that number.

A 92% activation chart is a fine appendix slide. It should never be the only slide in the room when the CFO asks what changed.
