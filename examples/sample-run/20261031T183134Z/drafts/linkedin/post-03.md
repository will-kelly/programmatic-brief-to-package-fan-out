Same company. Same pilot. Two teams.
One team walked into renewal with a case. The other walked in with nothing.

A director at a fintech ran a two-team coding assistant pilot. In week one, one of the teams agreed to track review turnaround.

At renewal, that team's numbers made the case. The other team's numbers didn't exist.

The survey data points the same way, though it can't tell you which causes which. In the Northfield Developer Survey 2026, weekly active use in pilots with no success measure fell from 78% in month one to 41% by month three. In pilots that had one, it went from 80% to 66%.

Both groups dropped. The pilots with a measure dropped far less.

To copy the week-one move:

Pick one metric the team already feels, like review turnaround.

Record its current value before anyone gets a seat.

Name one person who owns reporting it until the renewal decision.

None of this needs a dashboard project. It needs an agreement in the first week, while people still remember what the work looked like before the tool.

If you're splitting a pilot across teams, the team without a measure won't hand you a weaker case at renewal. It will hand you no case at all.
